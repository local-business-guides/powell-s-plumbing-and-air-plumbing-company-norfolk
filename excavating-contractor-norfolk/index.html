Scope, timing, and terms to know

Excavation work can vary from a limited dig to a larger site preparation job. The first step is to clarify the scope and any access or safety concerns. Before digging, ask how underground utility locations will be handled and what preparation you need to make on your property.

Key facts to discuss:
- What it covers: The requested digging, soil movement, and any agreed site preparation. Confirm the exact work included before scheduling.
- Typical timing: There is no single timeframe for every job. The size and depth of the work, access, site conditions, and any required coordination can affect the schedule.
- Cost factors: The amount of work, equipment needs, access, soil or site conditions, and related work can affect the cost. Ask for a job-specific explanation rather than relying on a general estimate.

When comparing providers, give each one the same project description. Ask what is included, what could change the schedule or scope, and how the work area will be left. A clear answer is more useful than a broad promise.

A few terms may come up. Site preparation means getting an area ready for the next stage of work. Access is the route and space available for equipment and workers. Utility locating is identifying underground service lines before digging; ask what steps apply to your job.
